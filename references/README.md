# References

Citation list only (no PDFs in this repository). A reference is cited in the Lean sources only
for statements that were actually looked up in it; where a text was not available, this is said.

* [BC] O. Brinon, B. Conrad, *CMI summer school notes on p-adic Hodge theory* (preliminary
  version, June 24, 2009), https://math.stanford.edu/~conrad/papers/notes.pdf.
  Used: §2.1 (Proposition 2.1.2, `ℂ_K^H = \hat{L}`, proved from [Ax, Prop. 1]),
  §2.2 (Theorem 2.2.7, Tate–Sen), §2.3 (Lemma 2.3.1, Serre–Tate), §2.4 (Proposition 2.4.4),
  §5 (formalism of admissible representations), §14 (Tate–Sen formalism; §14.3, proof of
  Theorem 14.3.4: `H¹(Gal(K_∞/K), \hat K_∞) ≃ H¹(Gal(K_∞/K), K) ⊕ H¹(Gal(K_∞/K), X)`),
  §13.1 (Theorem 13.1.2, Tate's almost-étaleness theorem), §13.7 (Exercise 13.7.2, descent of
  finite extensions of `K_∞` to finite layers; Exercise 13.7.4, Faltings' proof: parts (1)–(6),
  formalised with `d = 0`; parts (3) and (6) say "δn→∞", but the inequality of part (6),
  `δ_n − δ_{n+1} ≥ β_n − (d+1)(δ_n − δ_{n+1})`, gives `δ_n → 0`, which is what is used), §14.1 (Lemma 14.1.3, axiom (TS1)).
* [Serre] J.-P. Serre, *Local Fields*, Ch. III, §6, Prop. 12 (rings of integers of complete DVR
  extensions with separable residue extension are monogenic) and §7, Prop. 14 (annihilator of
  `Ω¹` is the different). **Not available locally**; cited only as quoted in [BC, Ex. 13.7.1]
  ("[44, Ch. III, §7, Prop. 14]"); the monogenicity statement used is proved from scratch in
  `AlmostEtale/Mono.lean`.
* [Ax] J. Ax, *Zeros of polynomials over local fields — the Galois action*, J. Algebra 15
  (1970). **Not available locally**; cited only through [BC, proof of Prop. 2.1.2], which
  quotes [Ax, Prop. 1] in the form "for each n there exists y_n ∈ K̄^H with
  v(x_n − y_n) ≥ n − p/(p−1)²". The proof formalised here is our own write-up of the
  standard argument (Hasse derivatives of the minimal polynomial), see `Blueprint.md`.
* [Tate] J. Tate, *p-divisible groups*, Proc. Conf. Local Fields (Driebergen, 1966), Springer
  1967, 158–183. Scanned copy (OCR) consulted locally since 2026-10-18 (not committed; from
  https://www.sas.rochester.edu/mth/sites/doug-ravenel/otherpapers/tate-pdiv-ocr.pdf).
  Used: §(2.2), Proposition 2 (pp. 164–165: the discriminant of `A_v` is generated by
  `p^{n v p^{hv}}`, `n = dim G`, `h = ht G`; proof via multiplicativity in exact sequences, the
  connected–étale sequence, and Lemma 1 `disc(𝒜/𝒜') = N(a)` for `𝒜 = R[[X]]` over itself via
  `[p^v]`, with `a = p^{vn}·unit` from invariant differentials; Lemma 1 is reduced to an
  unproved trace map citing Hartshorne, *Residues and Duality*); §(2.3), Proposition 3
  (p. 166, `n + n' = h`, via `0 → Ker F → Ker p → Ker V → 0` over the residue field). Earlier
  statements attributed to [Tate] were taken from [BC], [Stix], [Hong], [LocAn].
* [Haines] T. Haines, *Notes on Tate's p-divisible groups*, https://math.umd.edu/~tjh/Tate_pdiv_notes.pdf
  (consulted locally, not committed). Used: §2.3 (pp. 5–7): Lemma 2.3.1 (discriminants of tensor
  products, transitivity; stated without proof), Lemma 2.3.2 (`disc H = (disc H')^{m''}
  (disc H'')^{m'}`, proof assuming (i)–(ii)), §2.3.2 (Tate's Prop. 2 assuming Lemma 1), §2.3.3
  and Lemma 2.3.3 (`δ_{A/A'} = N_{A/A'}(τ)` when `τ ↔ Tr` under `A ≅ Hom_{A'}(A, A')`).
* [Lipnowski] M. Lipnowski, *p-divisible groups*, lecture 9 of B. Conrad's seminar on the
  Mordell conjecture, http://virtualmath1.stanford.edu/~conrad/mordellsem/Notes/L09.pdf
  (consulted locally, not committed). Used: Appendix (pp. 17–19): Claim 2 (`disc(𝒜/𝒜') =
  N(det(∂[p^v]_i/∂X_j))`), Claim 2'' (`disc = N(τ)` for `Hom_A(R, A) ≅ R`, `Tr ↦ τ`, full proof),
  Claim 2' (`Hom_O(O', O) ≅ O'` with `Tr ↦ det(∂f_i/∂T_j)` for `O' = O[[T]]/(f)`, `f` regular,
  from Tate's Thm. A.3 in the appendix to Mazur–Roberts, *Local Euler characteristics*, Invent.
  Math. 9 (1970), **not available locally**).
* [Stacks] The Stacks Project, chapter *Discriminants and Differents* (consulted locally,
  not committed): Tags 0BWC (Tate's lemma), 0BWD, 0BWH (quasi-finite complete intersections:
  the different is `det(∂f_i/∂x_j)`), 0BVZ (Kähler different of a complete intersection),
  0BWA (norm of the different is the discriminant), 0BJF (étale iff discriminant is trivial).
* [Stix] J. Stix, *A course on finite flat group schemes and p-divisible groups*, Heidelberg,
  summer 2009, https://www.starkerstart.uni-frankfurt.de/115677822/stix_finflat_grpschemes.pdf.
  Used: §11 (Tate's Hodge–Tate decomposition, after [Tate, §4] and Shatz): §11.2.2 (Cartier
  duality for `p`-divisible groups, the perfect pairing `T_p(G) × T_p(G^D) → ℤ_p(1)`,
  Corollary 85), §11.3 (logarithm, Lemma 86, Corollary 87), Lemma 82 (formal section of
  `G → G^{et}`), Proposition 89, Theorem 91 (`G(Λ) ≅ Hom_{Gal}(T_p G^D, μ_{p^∞}(ℂ))`,
  `t_G(K) ≅ Hom_{Gal}(T_p G^D, ℂ)`), Corollaries 92–94 (Hodge–Tate decomposition).
* [Hong] S. Hong, *Notes on p-adic Hodge theory*,
  https://math.arizona.edu/~serinh/teaching/Notes%20on%20p-adic%20Hodge%20theory.pdf.
  Used: Ch. II §2.2 (Definition 2.2.3 formal group laws, Example 2.2.4 `Ĝ_m`, Lemma 2.2.5,
  Example 2.2.8 `[n]_µ`, Definition 2.2.9, Proposition 2.2.10 `µ[p^∞]` with `A_v = A/[p^v]_µ(I)`),
  §3.2 (Proposition 3.2.3 (2), Corollary 3.2.4, Proposition 3.2.7), §3.3 (Lemma 3.3.2,
  Lemma 3.3.3, Definition 3.3.4, Proposition 3.3.6 (1)–(4); (2) is stated for `λ ≥ 1` and fails
  for `p = 2`, `λ = 1`; we use `λ > 1`), Proposition 3.4.7, Example 3.4.8;
  Ch. II §3.4 (Proposition 3.4.6: `T_p(G) ≅ Hom(T_p(G^∨), ℤ_p(1))`; Proposition 3.4.9,
  Theorem 3.4.10, Corollary 3.4.11, Theorem 3.4.12: Hodge–Tate decomposition
  `Hom(T_p G, ℂ_K) ≅ t_{G^∨}(ℂ_K) ⊕ t_G^*(ℂ_K)(−1)`), §3.5 (Proposition 3.5.1, stated there
  without proof: the discriminant of `A_v` is generated by `p^{d v p^{h v}}`, proof sketched
  with reference to Haines' notes; Lemma 3.5.2, Proposition 3.5.3, Theorem 3.5.4 = Tate's full
  faithfulness `Hom(G, H) ≅ Hom(G_K, H_K)`, Corollary 3.5.5 `≅ Hom_{ℤ_p[Γ_K]}(T_p G, T_p H)`).
* [LocAn] S. Mochizuki, *The local pro-p anabelian geometry of curves*, Invent. Math. 138
  (1999). The consumer of this repository (Theorem A).
